Lessons from the Front Line

What we built

  • An in-house tax engine: 100+ countries, 13,000+ US jurisdictions
  • Embedded in the Stripe financial platform: checkouts, subscriptions, invoices, platform infrastructure
  • Seven lessons learned building it
  • They apply to any tax technology you build or implement

Lesson 1: Global ambition meets local reality

  • Your assumptions will be wrong — move fast anyway, but build for it
  • Clarity won't come: make rules configurable, and where you can't, make an educated guess
  • Prioritize by revenue, volume and enforcement exposure, and document the gaps you accept

Lesson 2: What you need to unlearn as a tax specialist

Good tax lawyers make bad tax software

  • Every exception matters → Build for the volume; give the long tail a fallback
  • Zero risk → Triage risk; not shipping is a risk too
  • Minimize non-compliance → Count the cost of compliance too
  • Wait for certainty → Decide with ~70% of the information; keep decisions reversible

Lesson 3: The data model decides everything

  • A wrong data model creates workarounds on workarounds, until nobody understands the data
  • Design for problems you've never seen: what if jurisdiction boundaries change? What if one transaction carries several taxes instead of one?
  • Your scope drives your complexity: model for the business you have and where it's heading
  • Test any system against your hardest jurisdictions, not your easiest

Lesson 4: Tax doesn't exist in isolation

  • Tax belongs in the design, not bolted on afterwards
  • Understand the commercial reality: the product architecture sets your most important constraints
  • Some constraints can't be removed, and no amount of tax reasoning will change them
  • Where you can't change the product, design tax around it

Lesson 5: AI is a tool, not a destination

  • Rule #1 of machine learning: don't always use machine learning
  • Match the model to the task; routine work rarely needs a frontier model
  • Every agent adds risk; prefer workflows where the task allows
  • AI makes output cheap and checking expensive, so every output needs an owner

Lesson 6: Measure outcomes, not activity

  • Much of tax's value is invisible: work that prevents or deprioritizes something leaves no trace
  • When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure
  • Even "accuracy" is ambiguous: accurate against the law, or against your own decisions?
  • Start with two or three outcome metrics, and watch what people optimize for

Lesson 7: The job with no job description

  • Work changes faster than job descriptions
  • Functional homes own recurring work; missions handle temporary, cross-functional work
  • Work can be fluid; accountability can't
  • Staff missions with flexible people who are willing to learn

Key takeaways

  • Don't chase 100% accuracy — decide which risks you accept
  • Unlearn the instincts that make you a good adviser but a bad builder
  • Get the data model right early
  • Understand the commercial reality, and get into the design early
  • AI is a transformation tool, not an end state
  • Measure outcomes, not activity
  • Your job is to solve problems, and problems will keep changing, so your job will too
